ingredients:
  - 1/3 cup long-grain white rice
  - 1/4 cup extra-virgin olive oil
  - 4 medium carrots, finely chopped
  - 1 large yellow onion, finely chopped
  - Kosher salt and black pepper, to taste
  - 2 (15 oz) cans chickpeas, divided
  - 3/4 cup fresh dill, chopped, divided
  - 5 garlic cloves, finely chopped
  - 2 tablespoons tomato paste
  - 2 teaspoons dried oregano
  - 1/2 teaspoon ground coriander
  - 1/2 teaspoon ground turmeric
  - 1/4 to 1/2 teaspoon red pepper flakes
  - 4 cups vegetable broth
  - 3 oz baby spinach or kale
  - 1/2 cup unsweetened plant-based milk
  - 1 large lemon, zested and juiced
  - 1/4 cup nutritional yeast
  - 1 tablespoon white miso

## Method

1. Rinse the rice in a fine-mesh sieve until the water runs nearly clear; drain. Drain and rinse the chickpeas, keeping the two cans separate.
2. Heat the olive oil in a large saucepan over medium-high heat. Add the carrots and onion with a pinch of salt and cook until softened and lightly browned, about 10 minutes.
3. Add the garlic and cook for 2 minutes, stirring often. Stir in the tomato paste, oregano, coriander, turmeric, and red pepper flakes and cook for 1 to 2 minutes, until darkened, deglazing with a splash of broth if needed.
4. Add the rinsed rice and toss to coat. Stir in one can of chickpeas, salt, and pepper, then pour in the vegetable broth and half of the dill. Bring to a boil.
5. Reduce the heat to low, cover, and simmer for 15 minutes, stirring once or twice.
6. Meanwhile, roughly chop the spinach. Blend the remaining can of chickpeas with the plant-based milk, lemon zest, 2 tablespoons of the lemon juice, nutritional yeast, miso, 1 teaspoon salt, and pepper until smooth and creamy.
7. Stir the chickpea puree and spinach into the soup and simmer for 1 to 2 minutes, until thickened and the greens have wilted.
8. Remove from the heat and stir in the remaining dill and 1 tablespoon lemon juice. Taste and adjust the seasoning, then serve with a drizzle of olive oil.

> Swap in white beans for extra creaminess, or use basil or parsley in place of the dill. Leftovers keep in the fridge 5 to 6 days, or freeze in individual portions for 3 to 4 months; brighten with a little extra lemon zest, juice, and olive oil when reheating.
