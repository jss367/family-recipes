ingredients:
  - 1 cup split yellow lentils (moong dal)
  - 1 to 2 tablespoons coconut oil
  - 1 medium yellow onion, diced
  - 4 garlic cloves, finely chopped
  - 1-inch piece fresh ginger, grated
  - 1 serrano pepper, finely chopped
  - 1 teaspoon Madras curry powder
  - 1 teaspoon garam masala
  - 1/4 teaspoon ground coriander
  - 1/4 teaspoon ground turmeric
  - 2 1/2 to 3 1/2 cups water
  - Kosher salt and black pepper, to taste
  - 14.5 oz crushed or diced tomatoes, one can
  - 1 handful fresh cilantro, chopped
  - 1 1/2 to 2 tablespoons coconut oil, for the tadka
  - 1 teaspoon black mustard seeds
  - 1/2 teaspoon cumin seeds
  - 1 to 2 pinches asafoetida (optional)
  - About 10 fresh curry leaves
  - 1 to 2 dried red chile peppers (optional)
  - Basmati rice or flatbread, coconut yogurt, and sliced red onion, for serving (optional)

## Method

1. Pick over the lentils to remove any debris, then soak in cold water for 15 minutes and drain.
2. Heat the coconut oil in a large sauté pan over medium-high heat. Add the onion with a pinch of salt and cook for 5 to 7 minutes, until softened and golden.
3. Add the garlic, ginger, and serrano pepper and cook for 60 to 90 seconds, until fragrant. Stir in the curry powder, garam masala, coriander, and turmeric and cook for 30 seconds.
4. Pour in the water and drained lentils along with 1 teaspoon salt and pepper, and stir to combine.
5. Bring to a boil, then reduce the heat, partially cover, and simmer for about 30 minutes, until the lentils are fully cooked, stirring occasionally and adding more water if needed.
6. Stir in the tomatoes and cook for 4 to 5 minutes, until broken down. For a creamier texture, blend part of the dal with an immersion blender. Stir in the cilantro and adjust the seasoning.
7. For the tadka, heat the coconut oil in a small saucepan over medium heat. Add the mustard seeds and swirl until they pop, then add the cumin seeds and cook a few seconds until darker. Add the asafoetida, curry leaves, and dried chiles, and swirl for 15 to 30 seconds, until fragrant. Remove from the heat right away.
8. Pour the tadka over the dal and serve with rice or flatbread, yogurt, and sliced red onion, if desired.

> Split red lentils, toor dal, or chana dal all work in place of moong dal — just adjust the cooking time as needed. Store the dal and tadka separately in the fridge for up to 5 days, and make a fresh tadka when reheating; the dal alone also freezes well for up to 6 months.
