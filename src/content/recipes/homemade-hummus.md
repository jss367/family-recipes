ingredients:
  - heading: For the hummus
    items:
      - 8 oz (227 g) dried chickpeas (about 1 cup + 2 tablespoons)
      - 1 teaspoon baking soda, divided
      - Generous 3/4 cup (about 185 g) good-quality tahini
      - 6 tablespoons freshly squeezed lemon juice, plus more to taste
      - 3 garlic cloves, chopped
      - 1 teaspoon ground cumin, plus more to taste
      - 2 teaspoons Diamond Crystal kosher salt, divided (1 heaping teaspoon for blending, 1 teaspoon for cooking the chickpeas)
      - Freshly cracked black pepper
      - 6 to 10 tablespoons (90 to 150 mL) ice water
  - heading: For the fried garlic-lemon topping (optional)
    items:
      - 1/3 cup (75 g) good-quality extra-virgin olive oil
      - 5 large garlic cloves, thinly sliced
      - 4 to 6 strips lemon peel, about 2 inches long
      - Flaky sea salt or kosher salt
  - heading: For serving
    items:
      - 1 handful flat-leaf parsley, chopped
      - Paprika, Aleppo pepper, or sumac
      - Warm pita or lavash bread

## Method

1. **Soak** the **chickpeas** in cold water with 1/2 teaspoon of the **baking soda** for 8 hours or overnight. Drain and rinse.
2. Put the soaked chickpeas in a medium saucepan with the remaining 1/2 teaspoon **baking soda** and 6 cups (1.4 L) **water**. Bring to a boil over medium-high heat, cover, and keep at a rapid simmer for 30 minutes.
3. Uncover, add 1 teaspoon **kosher salt**, and cook 10 to 15 minutes more, until the chickpeas are very soft and mash easily. Drain well.
4. Blend the hot chickpeas in a food processor for 1 to 2 minutes until smooth, scraping down the sides as needed.
5. Add the **tahini**, **lemon juice**, **garlic**, **cumin**, 1 heaping teaspoon **kosher salt**, and **black pepper**. With the processor running, stream in the **ice water** 1 tablespoon at a time. Check the texture after 6 tablespoons and add more if you want it looser. Taste and adjust the salt, garlic, cumin, and lemon juice.
6. For the topping, set a fine-mesh sieve over a bowl. Warm the **olive oil** in a frying pan over medium heat, add the sliced **garlic**, and cook, stirring often, for 2 to 2 1/2 minutes until just golden. Don't let it brown or it turns bitter. Add the **lemon peel** and cook 30 seconds more.
7. Strain the oil into the bowl. Move the garlic and peel to a plate and sprinkle with **flaky salt**.
8. Spread the hummus in a shallow, lipped bowl and swirl the surface with the back of a spoon. Spoon the garlic-infused oil over the top, then add the fried garlic and lemon peel, **parsley**, and a sprinkle of **paprika** or **Aleppo pepper**. Serve with warm **pita**.

> Canned chickpeas work in a pinch: use 2 (15 oz) cans, drained and rinsed, and simmer them in water with 1/2 teaspoon baking soda for about 20 minutes until they start to fall apart, then continue from step 4. The result is a little less smooth. Use tahini made from hulled sesame seeds, since unhulled tastes bitter. For milder garlic, start with 1 clove or soak it in the lemon juice for 10 to 15 minutes. If using table salt, sea salt, or Morton kosher salt, start with half the amount. Peeling the cooked chickpeas (rub them between paper towels) gives the smoothest texture but is optional. Use only the yellow part of the lemon peel, not the bitter white pith. Best served warm or at room temperature. Keeps in the fridge up to 4 days under a thin layer of olive oil, and freezes with some change in texture.
