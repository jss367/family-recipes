      - 1/4 cup water
      - 1/2 vegetable bouillon cube, crushed (or a splash of vegetable stock)

      - 2 teaspoons maple syrup (or agave)
      - 1 teaspoon ground cumin

      - Fresh cilantro and avocado, for serving (optional)

## Method

1. Stir together the **garlic** and **olive oil** in a small bowl, then brush it over both sides of the **bread**.
2. Toast the bread in a large pan over medium heat until golden on both sides, lowering the heat if the garlic starts to brown too fast.
3. Meanwhile, combine the **navy beans**, **tomato paste**, **water**, **bouillon**, **chipotle adobo sauce**, **maple syrup**, **cumin**, and **onion powder** in a small saucepan over medium heat. Cook for about 5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until heated through and saucy.
4. Spoon the beans over the garlic toast and top with the **yogurt**, **pickled jalapeños**, and **cilantro and avocado** if using.

> Navy beans are also called haricot or Boston beans — any cooked bean works here, though cannellini and kidney beans are good swaps. Look for a chipotle sauce made from chipotle peppers, water, vinegar, and spices rather than a mayo-based one. The beans keep in the fridge for up to 4 days, but the toast is best eaten right away.
