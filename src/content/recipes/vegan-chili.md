ingredients:
  - 3 tablespoons olive oil
  - 1 large yellow onion, diced
  - 6 garlic cloves, finely chopped
  - 2 jalapeños, diced
  - 2 tablespoons tomato paste
  - 4 tablespoons chili powder
  - 1 tablespoon ground cumin
  - 1 1/2 teaspoons smoked paprika
  - 1 tablespoon Mexican oregano
  - 3/4 cup dry red wine
  - 2 cups vegetable broth
  - 2 (15 oz) cans pinto beans, drained and rinsed
  - 1 (15 oz) can navy beans, drained and rinsed
  - 2 tablespoons cocoa powder
  - 2 bay leaves
  - 1 1/2 tablespoons tamari or soy sauce
  - 2 chipotle peppers in adobo, plus 1 tablespoon adobo sauce
  - 28 oz whole peeled tomatoes, one can, crushed by hand
  - 1 1/2 teaspoons kosher salt
  - Black pepper, to taste
  - 1 1/2 tablespoons maple syrup, plus more to taste
  - 1 to 1 1/2 tablespoons lime juice
  - 1 teaspoon red wine vinegar
  - 3 tablespoons masa harina (optional)
  - 1 cup cilantro leaves and tender stems, chopped
  - Vegan sour cream, avocado, scallions, shredded vegan cheese, and pickled red onions, for serving (optional)

## Method

1. Heat the olive oil in a Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Add the onion with a pinch of salt and cook until deeply golden, about 10 minutes.
2. Add the garlic, jalapeños, and tomato paste and cook for 2 to 3 minutes, until the tomato paste darkens.
3. Stir in the chili powder, cumin, smoked paprika, and oregano and cook, stirring vigorously, for 30 seconds.
4. Deglaze the pot with the red wine, scraping up any browned bits from the bottom.
5. Add the broth, pinto beans, navy beans, chipotle peppers and adobo sauce, bay leaves, salt, pepper, cocoa powder, maple syrup, and tamari.
6. Crush the tomatoes by hand and add them along with their juices. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a gentle simmer.
7. Simmer uncovered for about 1 1/2 hours, stirring every 10 minutes, until thick and velvety. Remove the bay leaves.
8. If using, stir in the masa harina and simmer 3 minutes more to thicken further.
9. Stir in the cilantro, lime juice, and vinegar. Taste and adjust the maple syrup, lime juice, salt, and pepper as needed.
10. Serve with your choice of toppings.

> Use ancho chili powder for the best depth of flavor if you're not making your own blend from dried chiles. For a milder chili, use one jalapeño with the membranes removed and cut back the chipotle to one pepper with a teaspoon of its adobo sauce; add a dried arbol pepper or two for more heat. No red wine on hand? Swap in unsweetened red grape juice or extra broth. Chili tastes even better the next day, keeps in the fridge 5 to 6 days, and freezes well in individual portions.
