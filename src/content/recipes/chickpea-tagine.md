  - heading: For the tagine

      - 1 large yellow onion, diced

      - 1 cinnamon stick (optional)

      - 1 handful flat-leaf parsley or cilantro, chopped

## Method

1. Stir together the **ras el hanout**, **coriander**, **cinnamon**, **cumin**, **ginger**, **turmeric**, **cayenne**, and **cloves** in a small bowl.
2. Heat the **olive oil** in a Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Add the **onion** with a pinch of **salt** and cook until golden, 7 to 8 minutes, adding a splash of water if it starts to catch.
3. Add the **bell pepper**, **garlic**, and **cinnamon stick** and cook for 3 minutes, stirring frequently.
4. Stir in the spice mix and cook, stirring constantly, for 1 minute, adding a little water if the pan looks dry.
5. Pour in a splash of the **broth** to deglaze, scraping up any browned bits. Add the remaining broth, **frozen squash** (straight from the freezer), **chickpeas**, **bay leaves**, salt, **tomatoes**, and **dates** and bring to a boil.
6. Drizzle generously with olive oil, stir, and reduce the heat to medium-low so the pot simmers gently. Cover with the lid.
7. Simmer covered for about 20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the squash is tender and the mixture has thickened into a stew. If it's still loose, uncover and simmer a few minutes more.
8. Meanwhile, stir together the **yogurt**, **lemon juice**, coriander, and a pinch of salt for the yogurt sauce.
9. Let the tagine cool for 15 to 20 minutes, then stir in half the **lemon zest** and the lemon juice. Taste and adjust the seasoning.
10. Serve topped with the yogurt sauce, fresh **herbs**, and a drizzle of olive oil.

> Frozen squash cubes go in straight from the freezer, so there's no peeling, dicing, or oven. Use a real cinnamon stick if you have one — it makes a noticeable difference over ground cinnamon alone. Swap golden raisins or dried apricots for the dates, or white beans for the chickpeas. Leftovers keep in the fridge up to 5 days and freeze well; thin with a little water or broth when reheating.
